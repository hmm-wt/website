**The Necessity Doctrine, Chapter 2**
*2 May 2026*

---

Five categories. Not seven. Five domains where capital, regulatory obligation, and geopolitical urgency are converging on the same timeline.

Food security systems. Biomedical systems. Power systems. Autonomous systems. Critical materials and supply integrity. Every other "regulated sector" is a subset, a synonym, or a distraction.

> **What qualifies a Necessity**
>
> - **Universality:** statutory architecture in every OECD economy
> - **Acceleration:** measurable regulatory output increase 2024–2026
> - **Durability:** cross-border coordination, not single-jurisdiction policy
> - **Capital signal:** sustained deployment through the 2022–2024 correction
> - **Geopolitical anchor:** treated as critical infrastructure in national security planning

---

## Necessity, not opportunity

Opportunity is the word venture investors use for markets where a better product can win. Necessity is the word governments use for markets where a worse product cannot be allowed to fail.

Each Necessity is a domain where private failure has public consequence. That is why each accumulated regulatory architecture over decades. That architecture is the barrier the doctrine identifies.

Adjacent verticals fail one of the criteria. Edtech has architecture but no geopolitical anchor. Proptech has capital signal but minimal cross-border coordination. Most of what venture capital calls "regulated" is consumer-protected, which is a different and thinner barrier.

## Food security systems

Every OECD government now treats food production and traceability as critical infrastructure. The framing shifted between 2020 and 2024.

The FDA's Final Rule 204 on food traceability becomes enforceable 20 January 2026.[^1] The EU's Farm to Fork mandate pushes digital traceability onto every food business operator above SME thresholds by 2027.[^2] Japan codified the Food Supply Stability Act in 2024, designating eleven food categories as critical materials.[^3]

Global agritech and food-tech funding ran at multi-billion levels in 2025.[^4] The compliance software layer is growing faster than the application layer above it.

---

## Biomedical systems

Healthcare is the most regulated sector in every developed economy. The pace accelerated through 2024–2025.

The FDA finalised AI/ML medical-device guidance in late 2024, formalising the Predetermined Change Control Plan.[^5] EU MDR moved into hard enforcement. Japan's PMDA opened a fast-track for AI diagnostics. Singapore's HSA tightened software-classification licensing. Australia's TGA introduced a post-market AI surveillance framework.[^6]

Biomedical carries the highest in-thesis company count in the four markets: 1,269 classified companies, 19 T1 and 1 T2 regulations.[^7]

---

## Power systems

Grid stability, generation, storage, transmission. The framing moved from "energy policy" to "critical infrastructure."

The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism began customs declarations on 1 January 2026.[^8] The US Inflation Reduction Act has triggered hundreds of billions in announced deployment.[^9] AEMO's 2024 Integrated System Plan commits A$122 billion in transmission by 2050.

Energy venture funding held through 2024 and 2025 at levels comparable to financial services, against the wider pullback.

---

## Autonomous systems

Any system that operates physical assets without a human in the loop crosses a regulatory boundary.

The FAA finalised 14 CFR Part 108 covering routine drone operations beyond visual line of sight in 2025.[^10] Japan extended Level 4 drone authorisation. The IMO published the Maritime Autonomous Surface Ship Code. NHTSA's Automated Vehicle framework moved through consultation in late 2025.

The category captures more than vehicles. Autonomous warehouses, mining equipment, agricultural machinery, defence platforms. Each operates under sector-specific regulation being rebuilt for AI-driven decisions.

---

## Critical materials and supply integrity

Lithium, rare earths, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals. National security export controls in every major economy.

The US CHIPS Act allocated $52 billion subject to eligibility audits. The EU's Critical Raw Materials Act came into force May 2024, designating 17 strategic materials with 2030 supply targets.[^11] Japan's Economic Security Promotion Act designates eleven specified critical materials. Australia's Critical Minerals Strategy 2023–2030 committed A$4 billion in facility funding.[^12]

The supply integrity layer (track-and-trace, provenance, export-control compliance) is itself becoming a regulated category.

## What did not make the cut

Mobility outside autonomous systems. Real estate. Education. Retail. Generic enterprise software. Consumer health. Each has regulatory exposure but fails the universality, durability, or geopolitical-anchor test.

Chapter 3 introduces the deal filter. How to distinguish a company inside the regulatory perimeter from one that sells across it.
