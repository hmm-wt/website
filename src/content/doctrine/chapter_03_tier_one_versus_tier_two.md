# Tier 1 and Tier 2

**The Necessity Doctrine, Chapter 3**
*2 May 2026*

---

The deal filter is binary. Either the company sits inside the regulatory perimeter, or it sells across it. Tier 1 companies require statutory approval to ship. Tier 2 companies serve buyers who require statutory approval to operate. Everything else is unregulated and falls outside the doctrine.

The distinction matters because the barrier is built differently in each case. For T1, the barrier is the licence. For T2, the barrier is the institutional sales motion that grants access to regulated buyers. Both compound. Neither is built quickly.

> **The classifier**
>
> - **T1:** Company is the regulated entity. Examples: licensed lender, payments institution, medical device manufacturer, energy retailer, certified drone operator.
> - **T2:** Company sells to regulated entities. Examples: KYC software, clinical trial management system, grid management platform, pharma quality assurance tooling.
> - **Conservative classifier:** assigns T1/T2 only where regulatory exposure is structural to the business model, not incidental.
> - **Public empirical figure:** T1 + T2 combined captured **33.5%** of the $425B 2025 global venture deployment under that conservative classifier.[^1]

---

## Why a binary

Most venture frameworks tier by sector or by stage. The doctrine tiers by regulatory posture because that is the variable that predicts barrier strength.

A sector tier tells you whether the company is in fintech. A regulatory posture tier tells you whether the company has a UK Financial Conduct Authority licence, an MAS Major Payment Institution licence, or no licence at all. The first two are T1. The third, depending on what it sells and to whom, is either T2 or unregulated. The valuation, the sales motion, the unit economics, and the exit path are different for each.

The binary is also defensible empirically. The conservative classifier applied to 2025 global venture deployment counts only companies where regulatory exposure is structural, not incidental. A company that happens to sell into a regulated industry but does not differentiate on regulatory capability is unregulated. A company whose product cannot ship without a licence or cannot win deals without a compliance certification is T1 or T2.

---

## Tier 1: the licensed entity

Tier 1 companies operate inside the regulatory perimeter. They hold the licence, the permit, the certification, or the approval. They are the entity the regulator inspects.

Examples: a digital bank holding an MAS Digital Bank licence. A medical device company with FDA 510(k) clearance. A drone operator with an FAA Part 135 air carrier certificate. A renewable energy retailer with an AEMO market participant authorisation.

The barrier is the licence itself. Licences are slow to issue, expensive to maintain, and rare to lose once held. The compounding mechanism is regulatory familiarity. An entity that has cleared a Series A licensing review is materially more likely to clear the next jurisdiction's licensing review than a competitor without prior approvals.

T1 entry valuations carry a measurable premium even at seed. Capital concentrates in proven regulatory navigators because the optionality of the next licence is itself an asset. The premium widens with stage as the regulatory portfolio expands.

---

## Tier 2: the seller across the perimeter

Tier 2 companies serve T1 entities. They ship compliance software, monitoring tools, audit infrastructure, model risk management platforms, or any technical or operational substrate the regulated buyer needs to remain regulated.

Examples: KYC and AML platforms sold to banks. Clinical trial management software sold to pharmaceutical companies. Grid management telemetry sold to network operators. Pharmaceutical quality assurance tooling sold to drug manufacturers.

The barrier in T2 is the sales motion. Selling into regulated buyers requires institutional procurement processes, security reviews, regulatory references, and audit-ready deployment. Each of these is a sustained capability investment. Each gets harder to replicate as the buyer's regulatory exposure deepens.

T2 has a structural advantage that T1 does not. The regulatory burden compounds on the customer, not the vendor. As the buyer's regulatory obligations grow, the vendor's contract value grows. The vendor does not need to clear the licence; the vendor needs to be the obvious choice once the buyer has cleared the licence.

---

## What the binary excludes

Companies that operate adjacent to regulation but not inside it. Companies whose buyers are regulated but who do not sell on regulatory capability. Companies whose product passes through a regulatory environment but does not interact with the regulator.

A SaaS productivity tool sold to a hospital is not T2 unless it differentiates on HIPAA compliance and clinical workflow integration. A payments processor for retail merchants is T1 only if it holds the relevant payments licence. A telemedicine platform is T1 if it holds the clinical practice authorisation, T2 if it sells the platform to a clinic that holds the authorisation, and unregulated if it positions itself as a general consumer wellness app.

The exclusion is intentional. The doctrine is about durable barriers built by statute. Companies that are merely adjacent to regulation do not have those barriers. They compete on distribution, brand, and network effects, which is the older venture playbook.

---

## How the filter changes the deal

The same nominal company shows up differently when the filter is applied.

The unit economics are different. T1 has higher gross margins because the licence creates pricing power. T2 has stickier revenue because compliance switching costs are high. Unregulated has lower CAC because distribution is cheaper. The doctrine prefers the first two and discounts the third.

The exit path is different. T1 acquirers are strategic incumbents in the regulated industry; the price is set by the regulatory advantage rather than the technology stack. T2 acquirers are platform providers consolidating compliance capability; the price is set by recurring revenue durability and integration depth. Unregulated acquirers are generally horizontal SaaS rollups, which have priced down through 2024-2025.

The deployment timeline is different. T1 companies are slower to revenue but reach durable margin sooner. T2 companies have longer sales cycles but lower customer churn once landed. The doctrine assumes longer holding periods for both than for unregulated comparables, with the evidence in observed exit timing for regulated venture-backed companies.

---

## What this means for portfolio construction

The classifier sets the universe before any deal-specific judgement is applied. A regulated-sector thesis fund that does not classify by tier is not actually executing the thesis. It is choosing companies that look regulated rather than companies that are regulated.

Portfolio construction follows. A fund whose mandate is regulatory barriers should be majority T1 or majority T2 by deployed dollars, not by company count. Adjacent or unregulated investments should be the exception with explicit rationale, not the default with retroactive theming.
