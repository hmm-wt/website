**The Necessity Doctrine, Chapter 8**
*2 May 2026*

---

The window is 2024-2028. Not because of a five-year planning convention, but because of the convergence of specific timetables in the regulatory record. The EU's Digital Operational Resilience Act came into force 17 January 2025. The EU AI Act phases in across 2025-2027. The MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines transition period runs through approximately 2027. The U.S. FDA's Final Rule 204 is enforceable from 20 January 2026. Japan's AI Promotion Act and AI Strategy Headquarters were operational from late 2025. By 2028, every Necessity-aligned regulatory perimeter in every Five Necessities domain in each of the four target markets will have moved from drafting into enforcement.

That window is the period in which the structural advantage accrues to early movers. Companies that ship inside the perimeter during the build phase carry the procurement-reference and regulatory-familiarity assets through the enforcement phase. Companies that arrive after enforcement is settled face the same compliance burden without the structural lead time.

> **Why 2024-2028**
>
> - **DORA enforceable:** 17 January 2025 (EU)[^1]
> - **EU AI Act:** prohibitions February 2025; general-purpose AI obligations August 2025; high-risk system obligations August 2026; full applicability August 2027[^2]
> - **FDA Final Rule 204 traceability:** enforceable 20 January 2026 (U.S.)
> - **MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines:** transition through approximately 2027 (Singapore)[^3]
> - **Japan AI Promotion Act:** in force from June 2025; Strategy Headquarters operational September 2025[^4]
> - **EU CBAM:** definitive period from 1 January 2026
>
> Each item is statutory or near-statutory. Each carries enforcement consequence. The window is the overlap.

## What "crystallisation" means in this context

A regulatory regime crystallises when its enforcement architecture, its compliance expectations, and its market participants' adapted behaviour align. Before crystallisation, the regime is text. After crystallisation, the regime is the operating environment.

The crystallisation period is short and asymmetric. Companies that build during it inherit the residual ambiguity (which is why they need regulatory familiarity to navigate) but also inherit the procurement preferences and regulator relationships that compound after the regime settles. Companies that build after crystallisation face a stable but more competitive market.

In each of the four target markets, the crystallisation period sits inside the 2024-2028 window. The mechanisms differ by market. Japan crystallises through the AI Strategy Headquarters' coordination and METI guideline iteration. Australia crystallises through APRA's CPS 230 and AEMO transmission build-out. New Zealand crystallises through the MBIE Innovation Services transition. Singapore crystallises through the MAS AI guidelines transition and the stablecoin framework enforcement.

## The structural-advantage mechanism

A T1 company that holds a licence by 2026 has different optionality from a T1 company that applies for the same licence in 2029. Three differences are material.

First, regulator familiarity. A T1 entity that has cleared an inspection cycle has a working relationship with the regulator. The next licence application, the next jurisdiction expansion, the next product variant inside the existing perimeter all face lower friction because the regulator has prior knowledge. This is the compounding mechanism that turns a one-time licence into a multi-product platform.

Second, procurement reference. A T2 vendor deployed inside a regulated buyer during the build phase becomes the reference deployment cited in the buyer's regulatory submissions, internal audit reports, and peer-bank conversations. By the time the regime settles, the vendor is the assumed default. Reference status compounds at industry conferences, at regulatory consultation processes, and in cross-border procurement decisions.

Third, talent gravity. Senior compliance, regulatory affairs, and policy talent moves toward the firms that have done the work first. Once the regime settles, the talent market re-prices, and the firms that hired during the ambiguous phase have a senior team that the late entrants cannot replicate at the same cost.

## Where falsification sits

The window framing is testable. It is wrong if any of three observable conditions appears.

First, regulatory rollback. If a major statutory regime is repealed or materially weakened during the window (the EU AI Act stripped of its high-risk provisions, the FDA Rule 204 indefinitely suspended, MAS guidelines withdrawn), the structural advantage thesis collapses. The response would be to accept the rollback and re-price the framework.

Second, compliance commoditisation. If the compliance work itself becomes a low-cost commodity (cheaper than the regulatory advantage it produces), the barrier degrades. AI-driven compliance automation is the active counter-pressure here. The doctrine's view is that AI compresses the compliance task but does not compress the licensing pathway. The regulator-relationship asset is not a compliance task.

Third, exit market freeze. If strategic acquirers or IPO markets close to regulated venture-backed companies for an extended period, the exit path narrows and the carry economics collapse. This risk is real over short cycles. The structural response is the longer fund term and the active LP-secondary market.

None of the three conditions is currently active in observable form. Each is monitored.

## What the window does to portfolio construction

A fund with a 2024-2028 deployment window aligns with the crystallisation period. The investment period is the build phase; the harvest period is the enforcement phase. That alignment is the implicit calendar of the doctrine.

A fund with a deployment window outside this period faces different arithmetic. Pre-2024 deployments under-utilise the regulatory tailwind because the regime had not yet started building. Post-2028 deployments compete with established T1 incumbents and T2 reference vendors who hold the structural advantage. Both can produce returns; neither produces returns from the same mechanism the doctrine identifies.

The implication is not that the doctrine is invalid outside the window. The implication is that the mechanism is window-specific. The next two chapters examine what happens after the window closes, in the two scenarios that bracket the realistic range.

---

## The two branching paths

By 2028, the four target markets will have reached one of two outcomes. Either the regulatory architecture in each market continues to deepen and converges on common standards (path A: crystallisation across all four markets). Or the architectures diverge, with some markets tightening and others retreating to lighter regimes under fiscal or political pressure (path B: fragmentation).

Both paths produce returns for the doctrine, but through different mechanisms. Path A produces returns through standard-portfolio compounding: the assets accumulate value as the regime settles and procurement budgets grow. Path B produces returns through specialisation: the funds and companies that adapt to a multi-regime world capture the friction premium across regulatory boundaries.

The next two chapters describe each path in detail. Neither is forecast as more likely. Both are plausible. The portfolio construction implications differ.
