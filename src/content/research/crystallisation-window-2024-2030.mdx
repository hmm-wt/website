The fund's investment period is calibrated to a measurable regulatory window. Across the proprietary 22,217-company classified dataset, T1 regulated companies trade at a 1.12x round-multiple premium at seed and 1.53x at Series C. The cumulative T1-to-unregulated funding premium widens from 1.09x at pre-seed to 2.12x at Series D+. The thesis is to enter at near-parity and exit at premium.

That arbitrage closes once the compliance layer reaches commercial maturity. The investment window is the period before it does.

## The window is dated

Thirty-four in-flight regulations across the four markets carry effective dates between 2024 and 2030. Each one creates a category of work that did not previously exist. The fund's investment period is structured to deploy capital before the categories are filled.

The compliance layer reaches commercial maturity 24 to 48 months post-mandate. The window closes when it does. Specific anchors:

- **NCC 2025** takes mandatory effect May 2026 (Australia, construction).
- **PMDA and MLIT approval crystallisation** runs 2026 to 2028 (Japan, biomedical and autonomous).
- **EU Battery Regulation** battery-passport provisions apply from February 2027 (critical materials).
- **MAS AI governance** licensing framework arrives mid-2026 (Singapore, cross-cutting AI).

The window's peak density is 2026 to 2027. Sixteen of the 34 in-flight regs land in those two years.

## Why APAC seed and not US / EU seed

Regulation in the US and EU is largely incumbent advantage: the regulatory landscape was cleared a decade ago and seed entrants compete against incumbents that already cleared it. Regulation in APAC is regulatory advantage: NCC 2025 in Australia (May 2026 adoption), TSE governance reforms in Japan (2024 onward), Medsafe WAND in New Zealand, MAS sandbox extensions in Singapore. Each of these resets the field.

That arbitrage is absent in the US, where regulated-tech seed already trades at the premium.

## Per-Necessity sequencing

Within each Necessity, regulatory cycle times dictate deployment sequence:

- **Singapore's sandbox approvals** (6 to 12 months) allow earliest capital deployment and fastest regulatory validation.
- **Australia's frameworks** open the door to New Zealand via Trans-Tasman mutual recognition (FSANZ for food, Medsafe-TGA for health tech), making AUS the gateway investment for NZL expansion.
- **Japan's longer approval cycles** (12 to 24 months for PMDA, MLIT) are offset by the highest exit multiples in the portfolio. Japanese companies should be modelled at 7 to 10 year holding periods.

The fund does not need to predict which companies fill the categories. It needs to deploy before the categories are filled.

## What this is not

The window is not a forecast. Every regulation in the dataset above has a published effective date, a primary-source regulator publication, and a traceable parliamentary or executive record. The 34 in-flight regulations are enacted or in-parliament; expected-only regulations are tracked separately and not used as load-bearing thesis evidence.
