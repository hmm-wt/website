Regulators publish on a predictable rhythm. Draft, consult, enact, set an effective date, watch the operator scramble, publish the first enforcement action, settle into steady state. Each stage creates and closes commercial windows.

The fund deploys in stage four. The operator-build window. Before stage four the demand is hypothetical. After stage six the premium is priced.

## Where founders should engage by stage

**Stage 1. Consultation.** The regulator publishes a draft. Public comment opens. This is when the regulatory direction is most legible and most malleable. Founders who engage here are read as native by the regulator's office for the next ten years.

**Stage 2. Enactment.** Parliament or the executive ratifies. Effective date is set. Industry now has a deadline. The deadline is the demand. Talk to buyers about their compliance roadmap. Document the regulatory dependency in your investor materials.

**Stage 3. Effective date.** The rule comes into force. First reporting cycle, first audit, first licence application window opens. If your product is not in production by the effective date, you are competing for first-mover slots against operators who are.

**Stage 4. Operator build.** Operators race to ship compliant solutions. Buyers begin allocating compliance budget. First procurements close. This is the seed-to-Series A window. The fund's four-year investment period is calibrated to this band across the 34 in-flight regulations.

**Stage 5. First enforcement.** The regulator publishes the first enforcement action. The market validates that the rule has teeth. Compliance becomes commercial table stakes. The Series B / C round-size premium widens (the 1.53x figure runs through here).

**Stage 6. Steady state.** Compliance is table stakes. Late-cycle exits, larger acquirers, regulatory consolidation. If the company is still scaling here, the exit window is open. The acquirer is buying time, not technology.

## What the cycle is not

The cycle is a heuristic, not a contract. Real regulatory rollouts skip stages, reverse, or stall. Three failure modes are worth naming.

**Soft launches.** Some regulators publish a rule but defer enforcement indefinitely. The compulsion is on paper, not in audits. The fund treats unpublished-enforcement T2 rules as lower-conviction than published-enforcement T2 rules.

**Carve-outs.** SME exemptions, sandbox graduations, and grandfather clauses can compress the operator-build window. A 24-month build window inside a 12-month sandbox is the right buy.

**Regulatory reversal.** A new government or coordinated industry pressure can reverse a rule. The fund's R1 (regulatory reversal) risk is bound to the four-market diversification. A reversal in any single jurisdiction does not collapse the portfolio.

## How the fund times the cheque

The fund's primary deployment window is stages three and four. Effective-date crossing into operator build. The seed cheque buys time, credentials, and the first commercial reference. Companies that arrive too early (pre-enactment) carry a longer pre-revenue runway than the fund underwrites. Companies that arrive too late (post-first-enforcement) trade at the post-validation premium that closes the seed thesis.
