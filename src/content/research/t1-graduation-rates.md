The graduation question is empirical. T1 companies face a longer pre-revenue runway than unregulated peers. The question is whether the longer runway is compensated by higher graduation rates, larger rounds, and a higher probability of exit at term.

Every figure is sourced to the hmm Ventures classified universe (reference dataset 2026-04-08) or the funding-premium analysis (2024 four-market deal data).

---

## Why the rate compounds

Three mechanisms.

1. **Cleared milestones are sticky.** A TGA SaMD clearance, MAS Digital Bank licence, or PMDA SAKIGAKE designation does not expire on the same cycle as a product release.
2. **Cross-jurisdiction recognition.** A T1 company with FDA 510(k) is materially closer to TGA / HSA / Medsafe than a competitor starting from zero.
3. **Acquirer demand is structural.** Strategic acquirers cannot organically build the compliance layer at the pace the regulatory clock demands. Acquisition is the rational response.

## What this means at seed

A T1 seed entry at near-parity (1.12x) underwrites against a graduation curve that opens to a 1.53x Series C premium and a 2.22x exit-rate multiple. The capital-efficient window is the period before the first major regulatory milestone clears. That is the entry point the fund targets.

The case is observable, timestamped, and reproducible against the underlying dataset.

---

## What this is not

This is not a claim that T1 / T2 is the only path to venture returns. Unregulated companies produce outsized exits. The claim is narrower: inside the four-market regulated-sector seed thesis, the stage-graduation curve is materially steeper than the unregulated comparable.
