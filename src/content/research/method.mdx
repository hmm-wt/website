A regulated founder in Sydney, Tokyo, Auckland, or Singapore at seed will field term sheets from generalist developed-APAC funds, sector-adjacent regional CVCs, and occasional global strategics. The fund does not need to win every competitive seed process. It needs proprietary or semi-proprietary access to the subset where regulatory pathway design matters more than brand-name capital. The method is how the fund identifies that subset.

## The filter: T1 and T2

The two-tier binary takes the universe of developed-APAC technology companies down to the in-thesis subset.

**Tier 1: licence-as-barrier.** The company cannot legally ship without a regulator-issued licence, clearance, or approval. SaMD under TGA, PMDA, HSA, FDA. Grid-coupled storage participating in NEM, EMA, GX-ETS markets. AV stacks operating under LTA, MLIT, CASA. The licence is the moat. A competitor cannot reproduce the company's market position without reproducing the regulatory trail.

**Tier 2: compulsion-as-barrier.** The company cannot win deals without compliance evidence. Traceability under FSANZ, MAFF, FSAI. APRA-aligned commodities reporting. Mineral provenance under critical-materials regimes. The buyer is compelled by regulation to source from a compliant operator. Compliance evidence is the moat.

Companies that do not sit behind a T1 or T2 barrier are out of scope. The fund does not invest in generalist B2B SaaS, hardware-only plays without a regulatory pathway, crypto-native consumer applications, or companies whose only target market is the US or Western Europe.

Applied to the four-market universe, the filter produces a defined cohort.

The 6,505 figure is the smoking gun. It only reconciles to the four-market subset. Any reference to the in-thesis universe in this method ties to 19,416, not 22,217.

## The four markets

Each of the four target markets has built deep regulatory architecture in the five necessities and is in active enforcement transition through 2024 to 2030.

**Australia (35% allocation).** APRA, ASIC, AEMO, TGA, FIRB, ASX. R&D Tax Incentive at 43.5% refundable offset under A$20M turnover. A$3.7T superannuation pool turning toward unlisted growth. The deepest signal cohort.

**Japan (30%).** PMDA, MLIT, METI, FSA, TSE. CVC dominance. Ten named CVC partners active at Series A. TSE reform redirecting balance sheets to equity. The strongest financing access for T1.

**New Zealand (20%).** Medsafe WAND, Callaghan Innovation, Electricity Authority, MPI. Small market, regulator-as-partner pathway. Medsafe WAND accelerates SaMD approval; Callaghan co-invests in R&D. Volpara → Lunit at $193M USD in 2024 is the exit pattern.

**Singapore (15%).** HSA, MAS, EMA, IMDA, LTA, SFA. Regulatory arbitrage gateway for APAC. MAS sandbox, EDBI, Enterprise SG. 30x30 food security mandate by 2030.

## The compliance cycle

Regulators publish on a predictable rhythm. Draft consultation. Final rule. Phased commencement. Enforcement. Steady-state. The fund deploys in the operator-build window between final rule and first enforcement, when the compliance layer is being constructed and the demand curve is set by a regulator publication.

Companies that arrive earlier carry a pre-revenue runway seed cannot fund. Companies that arrive later trade at the post-validation premium that closes the seed thesis.

## Reading regulatory exposure

For a founder pitching the fund, the 30-second screen runs three questions:

1. **Which regulator's publication defines your demand curve?** Name the regulator, the document, the effective date. If the answer is "we expect regulation to follow", the company is too early.
2. **What does a competitor have to reproduce to displace you?** A licence (T1) or compliance evidence (T2). If the answer is brand, distribution, or speed, the company is out of scope.
3. **What is your operating window between the rule and the first enforcement?** Months, not years. If the window has closed, the company is too late.
