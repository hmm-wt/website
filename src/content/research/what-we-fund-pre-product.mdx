The cheque is $1.2M on average, written at pre-seed or seed, for around 15% ownership at entry. The fund will write 25 first cheques over four years. That is the construction. The screen is what decides which 25.

## The Five Necessities

The fund invests against five non-discretionary demand vectors. Demographic, physical, or geopolitical, not cyclical.

<NecessityBar />

**Biomedical Systems.** Aging populations across all four markets (Japan 29%, Australia 17%, Singapore 13% and accelerating, New Zealand 16%) are driving clinical-system demand that cannot be met by the current workforce without software.

**Autonomous Systems.** The autonomous-vehicle, maritime, and aviation regulatory calendars are now publishing approval pathways, converting a technology thesis into a regulatory one.

**Power Systems.** The energy transition is being legislated, not debated, and the compliance layer underneath each new building code and grid standard has not been built.

**Food Security Systems.** Global population growth and biosecurity risk are compressing supply chains that were designed for a different era.

**Critical Materials and Supply Integrity.** Supply-chain sovereignty mandates (EU CRMA, US IRA, Australia's Critical Minerals Strategy) are creating provenance and traceability obligations that did not exist three years ago.

## The T1 / T2 binary

A company is in-scope only if it sits behind a Tier 1 or Tier 2 barrier in one of the Five Necessities, in one of the four target markets.

**Tier 1. Licence-as-barrier.** A regulator has created or tightened a licensing regime, and the operational layer needed to comply with it is missing or under-built. The barrier is permission to operate.

**Tier 2. Compulsion-as-barrier.** A regulator has not created a licence, but has compelled an industry to do something it was not previously required to do, and the compliance layer is missing. The barrier is evidence of compliance.

## What the seed cheque buys

At pre-product, the fund is buying three things.

**A regulator-traceable demand signal.** A named regulation, a published effective date, a buyer with a compliance line item. Not a market-size deck. Not a TAM slide.

**A founder with an approval pathway, not an avoidance strategy.** The regulatory clock is the founder's friend. Founders who treat regulation as a feature ship faster than founders who treat it as friction.

**Embedded credentials over time.** A T1 seed company with TGA clearance is worth materially more than 1x its peer at Series A, because the acquirer would need to run an independent regulatory process to replicate the position. That replication cost, measured in years and hundreds of millions, is the exit premium.

## What we do not fund

Three categorical exclusions.

**Off-mandate sectors.** Financial services and fintech are fully off-mandate. Defence-tech and dual-use weapons applications are off-mandate. Both have real regulatory complexity. Neither is in scope.

**Companies outside the four markets.** AUS, JPN, NZL, SGP only. Not because other APAC markets do not have regulated industries; because the regulation map only produces a reliable result in jurisdictionally legible markets. Mainland China, India, Korea, Indonesia, Vietnam, and the Philippines fail one or more of the jurisdictional-legibility tests.

**Companies where regulation is incidental.** A company where the product would ship whether or not the regulation existed is not a regulated-industries thesis investment. The regulator's existence has to be load-bearing.

## What is fast and what is slow

Cycle times dictate cheque sequence within a Necessity. Singapore sandbox approvals run 6 to 12 months. Australia frameworks open into New Zealand via Trans-Tasman mutual recognition (FSANZ, Medsafe-TGA). Japan PMDA and MLIT pathways run 12 to 24 months and are offset by the highest exit multiples in the portfolio. Japanese portfolio companies should be modelled at 7 to 10 year holding periods, not 5 to 7.
