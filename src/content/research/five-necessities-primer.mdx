Necessity is the thesis. The fund invests against five non-discretionary demand vectors. Demographic, physical, or geopolitical, not cyclical. Each Necessity rests on a structural certainty that is enacted, not predicted.

This is the public primer. Each Necessity is what eats, heals, moves, powers, and supplies the developed-APAC economy.

## What sits behind each Necessity

**Biomedical Systems.** Aging populations across all four markets are driving clinical-system demand that cannot be met by the current workforce without software. Japan 29% over 65. Australia 17%. Singapore 13% and accelerating. New Zealand 16%. The compliance layer underneath every approved telehealth, every cleared medical device, and every electronic health record interoperability mandate is what the fund invests in.

**Power Systems.** The energy transition is being legislated, not debated. Building codes, grid standards, capacity investment schemes, hydrogen guarantees of origin, embodied-carbon disclosure. Each is a regulated demand vector with a published effective date. The compliance layer underneath the rules has not been built.

**Food Security Systems.** Population growth and biosecurity risk are compressing supply chains that were designed for a different era. FSANZ Standard 3.2.2A is the headline example: traceability mandates that route demand through compliant operators. The cumulative regulatory pressure across the four markets is meaningful and dated.

**Autonomous Systems.** Autonomous-vehicle, maritime, and aviation regulatory calendars are publishing approval pathways. MLIT, CASA, MPA, MOT, NZTA. Each is converting a technology thesis into a regulatory one. The fund's largest single-Necessity rule count sits here.

**Critical Materials and Supply Integrity.** Supply-chain sovereignty mandates (EU CRMA, US IRA, Australia's Critical Minerals Strategy) are creating provenance and traceability obligations that did not exist three years ago. The downstream compliance demand is structural, not cyclical.

## What the screen does

A company is in-scope only if it sits behind a Tier 1 (licence-as-barrier) or Tier 2 (compulsion-as-barrier) regulation in one of the five Necessities, in one of the four target markets.

Applying the screen to the four markets produces 117 Tier 1 and 27 Tier 2 regulations across the five Necessities. The Tier 1 share is steep: 81%. This is where the durable barriers sit.

The two largest buckets by rule count, Power Systems and Autonomous Systems, are also the two where the operational compliance layer is most under-built relative to the pace of new rulemaking.

## What this is not

The five Necessities are not industries the fund "likes." They are filters. Each rests on a structural certainty that an LP can verify against published demographic, physical, or geopolitical data. Not on a forecast about which technology trend wins.

The list does not change with market sentiment. It changes when the underlying certainty changes: when demographics turn, when grids re-stabilise, when food supply re-builds, when autonomy regulation reverses, when supply-chain sovereignty repeals.

None of those are happening.
