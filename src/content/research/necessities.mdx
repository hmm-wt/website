People keep eating, healing, moving, powering, and supplying themselves whether the market is up or down. The fund invests against five necessities that demographic, physical, and geopolitical certainty already underwrite. Regulation is the filter that tells the fund which companies are real.

## What sits behind each Necessity

**Biomedical.** Aging populations across all four markets are driving clinical-system demand that cannot be met by the current workforce without software. Japan 29% over 65. Australia 17%. Singapore 13% and accelerating. New Zealand 16%. The compliance layer underneath every approved telehealth, every cleared medical device, and every electronic health record interoperability mandate is what the fund invests in.

**Power.** The energy transition is being legislated, not debated. Building codes, grid standards, capacity investment schemes, hydrogen guarantees of origin, embodied-carbon disclosure. Each is a regulated demand vector with a published effective date. The compliance layer underneath the rules has not been built.

**Food security.** Population growth and biosecurity risk are compressing supply chains designed for a different era. FSANZ Standard 3.2.2A is the headline: traceability mandates route demand through compliant operators. The cumulative regulatory pressure across the four markets is meaningful and dated.

**Autonomous.** Autonomous-vehicle, maritime, and aviation regulatory calendars are publishing approval pathways. MLIT, CASA, MPA, MOT, NZTA. Each converts a technology thesis into a regulatory one. The fund's largest single-Necessity rule count sits here.

**Critical materials.** Supply-chain sovereignty mandates are creating provenance and traceability obligations that did not exist three years ago. EU CRMA, US IRA, Australia's Critical Minerals Strategy. The downstream compliance demand is structural, not cyclical.

## Why now

A measurable regulatory window is open across Australia, Japan, New Zealand, and Singapore. It closes when the compliance layer reaches commercial maturity.

<RegClockStrip />

Thirty-four in-flight regulations across the four markets carry effective dates between 2024 and 2030. Density peaks 2026 to 2027. Sixteen of the 34 land in those two years. Specific anchors:

- **NCC 2025** takes mandatory effect May 2026 (Australia, construction codes).
- **PMDA and MLIT approval crystallisation** runs 2026 to 2028 (Japan, biomedical and autonomous).
- **EU Battery Regulation** battery-passport provisions apply from February 2027 (critical materials).
- **MAS AI governance** licensing framework arrives mid-2026 (Singapore, cross-cutting AI).

## Why APAC and not US or EU

Regulation in the US and EU is largely incumbent advantage. U.S. and E.U. seed entrants compete against incumbents that cleared the regulatory hurdle a decade ago.

Regulation in developed APAC is regulatory advantage. NCC 2025 in Australia, TSE governance reforms in Japan, Medsafe WAND in New Zealand, MAS sandbox extensions in Singapore. Each resets the field.

Across the proprietary 22,217-company classified dataset, T1 regulated companies trade at a 1.12x round-multiple premium at seed and 1.53x at Series C. The cumulative T1-to-unregulated funding premium widens from 1.09x at pre-seed to 2.12x at Series D+. Enter at near-parity. Exit at premium. The arbitrage is absent in the US, where regulated-tech seed already trades at the premium.

## Why now and not later

The compliance layer reaches commercial maturity 24 to 48 months post-mandate. Companies that arrive too early carry a pre-revenue runway that seed funding does not support. Companies that arrive too late trade at the post-validation premium that closes the seed thesis. The capital-efficient band sits in the middle.

## What needs to hold

Three conditions through the investment period.

The five necessities remain regulated. None of the underlying drivers is reversing. Not aging. Not the energy transition. Not biosecurity. Not autonomy. Not supply-chain sovereignty.

The four markets remain jurisdictionally legible. Public regulator publications, traceable parliamentary records, enforceable IP regimes. All four are common-law or transparent civil-law systems. None has signalled a retreat.

A three-lane exit architecture remains available. Strategic acquisition (NA / EMEA), local and regional public listings, regional strategic M&A. The fund's return distribution does not depend on any single lane. Regulators are currently reinforcing all three.

## What this is not

The window is not a forecast. Every regulation in the dataset has a published effective date, a primary-source regulator publication, and a traceable parliamentary or executive record.

The five necessities are not industries the fund likes. They are filters. Each rests on a structural certainty an LP can verify against published demographic, physical, or geopolitical data. Not on a forecast about which technology trend wins.
