A single point estimate is not how an LP underwrites a fund. The same Monte Carlo distribution reads as four discrete tiers. Each tier is a percentile of the same simulation, not a separate forecast.

## The four-tier construct

**Target Case (P75).** The top quarter of simulated outcomes. The GP's stated return target. The number the GP underwrites toward.

**Base Case (median, P50).** The central tendency at benchmark methodology. The row half of simulations clear. ILPA-comparable.

**Downside Case (P10).** The worst-decile floor. The threshold 90% of simulations clear. The level the fund defends on.

**Bear (separate stress sim).** Coordinated parameter drift, not a percentile. AU and JP win-rate −3pp; winner multiples −20% across all four markets. The structural-stress overlay distinct from sampling variance.

## Why four tiers, not one

A single Target point estimate is the GP's pitch. A single Median is the benchmark-comparable read. A single Downside is the LP's defensible floor. A Bear stress is the structural overlay. Each answers a different LP question. Publishing one without the others misrepresents the simulation.

LP diligence converges on three questions: what is the upside that justifies the carry, what is the central tendency that matches benchmark methodology, and what is the floor at which the GP's reserves and concentration assumptions still hold. The four-tier framing answers all three with one distribution, one engine, one seed.

## The Return Bridge

The Bridge decomposes the gross return into three identifiable levers relative to an unweighted developed-APAC seed baseline. Each lever is empirically anchored.

**Lever 1. Survival advantage.** T1 companies fail less. The empirical anchor is the 13.41% T1 vs 6.03% unregulated all-exit differential on the four-market 2010-2015 cohort. The simulation reduces regional fail-rates accordingly. Full derivation in IM §6.2 and Appendix B §B.1.

**Lever 2. Win-rate advantage.** T1 companies win more. The empirical anchor is the 3.33x public-exit differential. The simulation lifts regional win-rates accordingly.

**Lever 3. Exit premium.** Winners exit at higher multiples. The empirical anchor is the 1.53x Series C round-size premium and the build-vs-buy calculus documented in the exit evidence research. The simulation scales winner exit multiples accordingly.

Levers compound rather than add. Bridge and simulation are two views of the same structural edge.

## Why Gross and Net differ

Gross is what the fund puts into companies. Net is what the LP receives after fund-level fees and the GP's profit share. The gap between Gross and Net is the standard seed-stage VC fee architecture applied over the fund's term.

Specific fund terms — management fee schedule, preferred return rate, GP catch-up mechanism, waterfall structure, carry percentage — are LP-tier and held in the private IM. The spread between Gross and Net sits inside the industry-standard band for seed-stage VC funds.

## Specific tier values

The Target / Median / Bear / Tail percentile values and the per-tier DPI, IRR, MOIC reads sit in the private Investment Memorandum, distributed only to qualified investors with a pre-existing relationship with the General Partner. Publication of specific simulated returns on a public surface is held back deliberately, consistent with the IM's US Regulation D Rule 506(b) reliance (no general solicitation) and the DFSA Professional Client framework.

## What this framework is not

It is not a forecast. Each tier is a percentile of a simulated distribution. The simulation is built from per-region outcome probabilities calibrated to developed-APAC seed-stage industry data, with the three levers applied to anchor against the fund's empirical thesis. The full lever derivation, sensitivity table, per-region parameter set, and tier values are held in the IM.

Past performance is not indicative of future results. Simulated returns are model outputs, not forecasts. Actual results may differ materially.
